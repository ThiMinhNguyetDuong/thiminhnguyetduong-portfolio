A live digital storytelling platform where audiences silently witness scripted chat conversations unfold in real time — built for UCLL as part of the #MovingMinds initiative, and currently in active use by the client.

## Project Overview

**Duration:** May 2026 – June 2026  
**Team:** Bachelor Project (5 members) — UCLL Applied Computer Science  
**Client:** UC Leuven-Limburg  
**My role:** QA & Testing Lead — E2E test suite, code reviews, PR reviews

Unsent Stories addresses a real gap: mental health topics like loneliness, social pressure, and online identity are hard for young people aged 14–24 to discuss, and traditional formats like lectures or theatre don't reach them where they actually spend their time. The platform delivers scripted stories through a familiar chat interface — users are silent witnesses to conversations between fictional characters, watching the story unfold in real time on their phones.

The concept is inspired by SKaGeN's WatchApp series, grounded in interviews with around 80 young people, and designed to spark reflection and open conversation around mental health themes in a way that feels natural rather than clinical.

## The User Journey

1. **Receive your ticket** — register and receive access to a live storytelling session
2. **Join the theatre** — enter the waiting room and wait for the story to begin
3. **Watch the story unfold** — scripted messages arrive in real time; switch between character points of view
4. **Reflect in the foyer** — after the show, discuss and share reactions with other viewers in a community space

## My Contribution — QA & Testing

I led the end-to-end testing effort using Playwright, responsible for designing the test strategy, writing the test suite, and conducting code reviews throughout the project.

**Test coverage:**
- ~85 test scenarios across 12 test files
- 4 browsers: Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Mobile
- 100% coverage of core flows: auth, theatre playback, admin panel, foyer

**Key engineering decisions:**

One challenge was test isolation — mutation-based tests that hit the backend API could leave stale data affecting other tests. I addressed this by always creating throwaway users per test and isolating each test in its own browser context.
